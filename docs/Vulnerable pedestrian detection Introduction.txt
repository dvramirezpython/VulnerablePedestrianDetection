Vulnerable pedestrian detection has raised a attention in recent research in computer vision, sensors, and edge computing due to the sensitivity of it application and the evolution of the Aritificial Intelligence (AI) algorithms, sensors, and edge hardware capabilities. A more comprehensive concept has arisen from recent studies: vulnerable road users (VRUs) —including pedestrians, cyclists, and micro-mobility users— using roadside infrastructure
Zhang et al provided an extensive review of technologies and methodologies for detecting VRU. Its objective is to synthesize current research on sensor modalities (e.g., cameras, LiDAR, RADAR), detection models, and datasets, with a focus on infrastructure-based systems rather than vehicle-mounted sensors. Key contributions include a detailed comparison of sensor types, a taxonomy of detection models (single- and multi-sensor, 2D and 3D, deep learning and traditional), and an evaluation of existing datasets, highlighting gaps in VRU subclass representation and critical scenarios. The survey also identifies emerging trends such as multi-modal sensor fusion, unified generalist models, and tracking-by-detection approaches. This work emphasizes the need for lightweight, scalable, and context-aware detection systems for vulnerable pedestrian in varyied conditions.

Song et al. presented a proactive approach to enhancing pedestrian safety, particularly for vulnerable groups such as the elderly. The authors highlight the alarming statistics of pedestrian fatalities in Korea, noting that over half of the victims in 2015 were elderly. This underscores the need for intelligent systems capable of analyzing pedestrian behavior and predicting potentially dangerous situations, especially around crosswalks. To address this, the researchers developed a system that integrates pedestrian and vehicle detection, tracking, and action recognition using deep learning. The core of their method is based on the Single Shot Multibox Detector (SSD) architecture, which is known for its efficiency in object detection. To improve processing speed and reduce computational load, they replaced the traditional VGG-16 backbone with MobileNet, a lightweight convolutional neural network that uses depthwise separable convolutions. This modification significantly reduced the processing time from 55 milliseconds (VGG-16) to just 12 milliseconds (MobileNet), making the system more suitable for real-time applications. The dataset used for training and evaluation includes footage from CCTV cameras installed at Seoul National University (SNU), collected over a three-month period. These videos were transmitted to a digital video recorder and an image analysis server at the Korea Electronics Technology Institute (KETI). The authors also utilized the COCO dataset for initial training but found that models trained on the real-world SNU dataset performed better in terms of detection accuracy, emphasizing the importance of domain-specific data. Despite its promising results, the paper has some limitations. It lacks detailed quantitative performance metrics such as precision, recall, or F1-score, which are essential for evaluating detection systems. Additionally, while the system includes action recognition capabilities, the paper does not provide a thorough analysis or validation of this component. Environmental factors like lighting, weather, and occlusions, which can significantly affect detection performance, are also not addressed.

Previously, some works have designed detection systems for vulnerable and not vulnerable pedestrian. Zahid et al. proposed a real-time pedestrian detection system that addresses the challenges of accuracy and speed in complex environments with occlusions and dynamic backgrounds. The authors employ a deep learning approach using the Single Shot Detector (SSD) framework combined with MobileNet, which leverages depthwise separable convolutions to reduce computational cost while maintaining high detection performance. The system is trained on the MS-COCO dataset, chosen for its rich annotations and diversity, and implemented using OpenCV within the Anaconda environment. Experimental results demonstrate a high detection accuracy of 99.99% for the "person" class and a throughput of 13–14 frames per second, making it suitable for real-time applications. The study highlights the model’s robustness in real-world scenarios, including crowded and cluttered scenes. However, limitations include reliance on a single dataset (MS-COCO), lack of evaluation on more diverse or occluded pedestrian datasets, and limited discussion on generalization across different environments or hardware constraints.

Similarly, Xiaoxiao et al. addressed the challenges of detecting vulnerable pedestrians—such as the elderly, children, and individuals with disabilities—who are often underrepresented in existing detection models. The authors identify three key limitations in the YOLOv7 architecture: loss of crucial information, weak correlation between high- and low-level features, and insufficient feature generalization. To overcome these, they propose enhancements including the SPAMPCSP module (which adds average pooling to capture global context), SE connections (to guide low-level features using high-level semantic information), and the SCELAN module (which improves feature fusion using spatial and channel attention). The model is trained and evaluated on the BG Vulnerable Pedestrian (BGVP) dataset, which includes 2,000 images categorized into four pedestrian types. Experimental results show that the improved model achieves a mean average precision (mAP@0.5) of 83.08%, outperforming the original YOLOv7 by 4.76 points, while maintaining real-time performance. However, limitations remain, particularly in detecting "Elderly without Disability" due to sample diversity and representation issues, and some misclassifications persist, indicating room for further refinement.

Sharma et al. introduced the Bowling Green Vulnerable Pedestrian (BGVP) dataset, specifically designed to address the underrepresentation of vulnerable pedestrian groups—children, elderly, and individuals with disabilities—in existing datasets. The dataset comprises 2,000 publicly sourced images with 5,932 annotated bounding boxes across four categories: Children without Disability, Elderly without Disability, With Disability, and Non-Vulnerable. The authors benchmark five object detection models—YOLOv4, YOLOv5, YOLOX, Faster R-CNN, and EfficientDet—on this dataset. Results show that YOLOv4 achieves the highest mAP@0.5 (0.7999), while YOLOX leads in mAP@0.5:0.95 (0.5616), particularly excelling in detecting the "With Disability" class. However, all models struggle with the "Elderly without Disability" category, likely due to ambiguity in age-based classification and sample diversity. Limitations include performance drops in low-light conditions and misclassifications between vulnerable and non-vulnerable groups. The study emphasizes the importance of data-centric approaches to improve fairness and accuracy in pedestrian detection for autonomous driving systems.

Other works have used other input sensors to improve the vulnerable pedestrian detection in adverse weather conditions. Palladin et al. introduced a novel sensor fusion framework designed to enhance 3D object detection in challenging weather conditions, particularly for vulnerable road users like pedestrians. The objective is to overcome the limitations of existing fusion methods that degrade under adverse conditions such as fog, snow, and low light. The key contributions include a transformer-based architecture that adaptively fuses data from RGB cameras, LiDAR, radar, and gated near-infrared (NIR) cameras using depth-guided blending and distance-aware attention mechanisms. The method employs early and late fusion strategies and a multimodal query generation process to improve detection robustness. Evaluated on the SeeingThroughFog dataset, SAMFusion achieves significant improvements, notably a 17.2 AP gain for pedestrian detection in dense fog and 15.62 AP in heavy snow at long ranges (50–80 meters), outperforming state-of-the-art methods. The dataset includes over 12,000 annotated samples across various adverse weather scenarios. Limitations include increased system complexity and reliance on multiple sensor types, which may pose integration and cost challenges for real-world deployment. Future work aims to extend the system to planning and uncertainty modeling for autonomous driving.

Dissanayake et al. presented a smart, IoT-based solution to improve pedestrian safety and urban traffic efficiency, particularly for vulnerable groups such as the elderly and disabled. The system integrates real-time pedestrian and vehicle detection using YOLOv8, emergency vehicle recognition via sound analysis, and weather-aware signal control to dynamically adjust crossing times. It classifies pedestrians into four categories and extends green light durations for vulnerable individuals, while also prioritizing emergency vehicles using audio features like MFCCs. The system was trained on datasets including Roboflow and COCO, and achieved promising results with mAP@50 scores of 73.08% for vehicles and 66.67% for pedestrians. Despite its strengths—such as scalability, cost-effectiveness, and real-time adaptability—limitations include challenges in detecting pedestrians in crowded scenes, reliance on sensor calibration, and potential scalability issues in high-traffic or extreme weather conditions. Future work aims to enhance detection accuracy, integrate multi-modal traffic, and validate the system through large-scale field testing.

Based on the previous efforts, new research opportunities have arisen to improve the vulnerable pedestrian detection and its applications. There are challenges in adapting onboard-trained models to roadside contexts with limmied hardware devices, like edge devices. In addition, algorithms that yield competentive results in both normal and adverse weather conditions are demanded. Aiming to bridge such gaps, we proposed a computer vision model trained with images representing both normal and adverse weather conditions and capable of executing in edge computing devices like Raspberry Pi 4 and 5. The main contributions of this work are:

1. A single model trained for vulnerable pedestrian detection and handling both normal and adverse weather conditions, urban and rural scenes, and diverse lighting and weather conditions without retraining 
2. The proposed solution is a lightweight and Edge-Deployable Architecture, designed efficiently for real-time deployment on edge devices (e.g., roadside units, traffic cameras) with limited processing power.
3. Ablation studies on existing benchmark datasets for VRU and particularly vulnerable pedestrian detection showing competentive results in both conditions.
